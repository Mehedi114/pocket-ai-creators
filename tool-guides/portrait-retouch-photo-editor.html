How to Retouch a Portrait — Light, Softening & Spot Brush Guide

Make small portrait adjustments while keeping control of the result. Pocket Helper provides local face detection to position a softening area, brightness and shadow controls, and a manual brush that blends a small blemish toward the surrounding colour. Original view makes comparisons easy.

How to use Face Retouch

  1. 01

    Upload a portrait. Wait for local face detection, or select the face centre yourself if no face is found.

  2. 02

    Adjust light and softening gradually. Enable the spot brush and click a small blemish; Undo removes the most recent spot correction.

  3. 03

    Compare Original and Edited, inspect skin and eye detail, and download a PNG or JPG.

A PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Put it to work

For a non-official profile photo taken in uneven lighting, slightly raise the shadows and soften a small area. Use a low-strength spot blend and compare the result with the original to retain natural texture.

Important limits

This is not Photoshop, skin reconstruction or an identity-photo validator. The brush blends neighbouring colour rather than restoring missing detail. Detection may miss faces; with multiple faces, the largest is selected. Input limit: 15 MB and 24 megapixels. Editing is capped at 2000 pixels; the smaller interactive preview does not set export dimensions. Do not use retouching where passport or ID rules prohibit it.

Common questions

Are portrait photos uploaded for face detection?

No. Detection runs in the browser using model files served by Pocket Helper. The photo is not sent to a face-detection API. The first use needs time to load the model, and speed depends on the device.
